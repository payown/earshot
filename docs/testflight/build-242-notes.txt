### Chapter 75 — Build 242

*The shelf stayed still*

**What changed**

Build 242 hardens two data paths that could make Earshot stall or crash. If
cleanup merges or deletes a duplicate episode while the player still holds it,
Earshot now unloads that episode before SwiftData invalidates the row. Play,
pause, resume, position, and speed updates also confirm that the saved episode
still exists before using it.

Cloud sync reconciliation now runs in its own background SwiftData actor instead
of on the main actor. Large libraries and incoming iCloud changes should no
longer compete with navigation, playback controls, or VoiceOver. The same data
still syncs. This build does not add analytics, change Earshot's data model, or
change its privacy behavior.

**What to test**

Start an episode, move around the app, refresh its podcast, and use play, pause,
and resume from Earshot, the Lock Screen, and Bluetooth controls. Unfollow a
show while one of its episodes is loaded. Earshot should stop cleanly, with no
crash or delayed crash.

If you use Earshot on more than one device, change an episode's played state,
position, queue status, or podcast settings on one device and confirm the other
catches up. During launch and sync, move through a large Library or Inbox with
VoiceOver. Focus and speech should remain responsive.

Kashe was halfway through a show when the app went quiet. Not paused. Gone. By
the time she reopened it, everything looked ordinary enough to make the crash
feel imaginary.

Later, her phone caught up with changes from the Mac while she moved through the
Inbox. VoiceOver kept speaking. The player kept its place. Nothing asked her to
wait while a shelf rearranged itself.

She tapped Pause, then Play.

“Still there,” Kashe said. “Good.”
